\section{Distillation framework}

\subsection{Teacher and student}

\headingindent
Classical knowledge distillation compresses a teacher into a smaller student
by matching softened class distributions \cite{hinton2015distilling}. Let $x$
be an input and let $C$ be the number of classes. The teacher and student
produce logit vectors $z_T(x),z_S(x)\in\mathbb R^C$, where the subscripts $T$
and $S$ identify the teacher and student. The component $z_{T,c}(x)$ is the
teacher logit for class $c$, and $z_{S,c}(x)$ is defined analogously. For a
temperature $\tau>0$, their softened class probabilities are
\[
    p_T^{(\tau)}(c\mid x)=\frac{\exp(z_{T,c}(x)/\tau)}
    {\sum_{j=1}^{C}\exp(z_{T,j}(x)/\tau)},\qquad
    p_S^{(\tau)}(c\mid x)=\frac{\exp(z_{S,c}(x)/\tau)}
    {\sum_{j=1}^{C}\exp(z_{S,j}(x)/\tau)}.
\]
Here $c$ is the class whose probability is evaluated and $j$ indexes all
$C$ classes in the denominator. A temperature above one exposes relative
probabilities among classes that may be almost invisible at $\tau=1$. Hinton
et al. multiply the soft-target loss by $\tau^2$ to compensate for the
temperature-dependent gradient scale.

% Our use differs from compression for deployment. The student is deliberately
% limited, and its failure to follow the teacher is part of the detector.
During
training, both teacher and student see only ID data. During evaluation, a scalar score, called OOD score, is
computed from the teacher, the student, or their difference.

\subsection{Training objectives}

\headingindent
The project implemented centered-logit mean squared error, soft and hard
cross-entropy, and Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence. To simplify notation, the
dependence on $x$ is omitted below. For $M\in\{T,S\}$, define the mean logit
$\overline z_M=C^{-1}\sum_{c=1}^{C}z_{M,c}$, and let
$\mathbf 1\in\mathbb R^C$ be the vector of ones. The centered-logit loss is
\[
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{MSE}}
  =\left\|\left(z_S-\overline z_S\mathbf 1\right)
  -\left(z_T-\overline z_T\mathbf 1\right)\right\|_2^2.
\]
The squared Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|_2^2$ measures the distance between the
two centered logit vectors. Centering removes any constant logit offset, which
softmax ignores. The mixed cross-entropy objective is
\[
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}
  =\alpha \tau^2 H\!\left(p_T^{(\tau)},p_S^{(\tau)}\right)
   +(1-\alpha)H\!\left(y,p_S^{(1)}\right),
\]
where $H(q,p)=-\sum_{c=1}^{C}q(c)\log p(c)$ is cross-entropy,
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ controls the balance between the soft teacher target and the
hard label, and $y$ is the one-hot ID class label. The first term transfers the
teacher's full class distribution; the second retains ordinary supervised
classification. We also used the forward KL objective
\[
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{KL}}
  =D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T\parallel p_S\right).
\]
Here $p_T=p_T^{(1)}$ and $p_S=p_S^{(1)}$ unless another shared temperature is
specified. The divergence is
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T\parallel p_S)
  =\sum_{c=1}^{C}p_T(c)\log\!\left(\frac{p_T(c)}{p_S(c)}\right).
\]
Minimizing it makes the student distribution approach the fixed teacher
distribution.

\subsection{OOD Scores}

\headingindent
OOD Score is oriented so that a larger value means more ID-like.
Raw distances, errors, and uncertainties are negated. We evaluated four score
families. Teacher-only scores serve as reference baselines.
